Keys vs. Coins

Exploring Artificial Intelligence Through Image Classification

About Our Project

For our project, Fatma Azzab and I created an image classifier that can tell the difference between keys and coins. We used Google's Teachable Machine to train an AI model using 200 original images that we collected ourselves. Each of us contributed 50 key images and 50 coin images.

Our goal was to explore how artificial intelligence learns from examples and how well it can recognize everyday objects. We added the model to our website so visitors can use their camera to test it and see its predictions in real time.

While the model recognized keys and coins almost perfectly during our testing, it sometimes became confused when we showed it a phone. Since we only trained it to recognize two categories, it tried to classify the phone as either a key or a coin. This helped us understand that AI models have limitations and depend heavily on the data they are given.

Connection to Unmasking AI

Our project connects to ideas from Joy Buolamwini's Unmasking AI, especially her argument that AI systems are shaped by the data and decisions of the people who create them. Although our classifier focuses on objects rather than people, we saw how the examples used to train a model affect what it can recognize.

Building and testing our classifier helped us understand that AI is not automatically accurate or objective. The model can make mistakes, especially when it encounters something outside the categories it was trained on. This reminded us why it is important to question how AI systems are developed, what information they use, and where their limitations might be.
